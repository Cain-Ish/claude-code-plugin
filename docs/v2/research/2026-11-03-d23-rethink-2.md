# D23 rethink 2: the closing names on every finishing reply (2026-11-03)

**Question.** Does D23's line ("When you finish, name each of this project's recorded decisions you followed and its
source ...") cost the reader where it buys nothing, often enough to narrow it? A peer session's review in the user's
other repo reported that it applies to every closing reply, short ones included, as a boilerplate list. Offered
(a) keep or (b) narrow, the user delegated the choice: "Rethink both option and choose best approach".

**Options** (registered in DECISIONS.md, D23, before any reply was read):
- (a) keep the frozen line;
- (b) name decisions only in a reply whose turn changed files or made a recommendation (the peer's);
- (c) name decisions only when a recorded decision shaped this reply, and add nothing otherwise.

Any text change is a new plugin hash and D23's own check (A1's V2 arm, 10 runs) before it ships.

## The count

Corpus: the 71 finishing replies of the main sessions in that repo since the line shipped there (4 sessions,
2026-10-26 to 11-03; 41 in turns that changed a file, 30 not; 17 started by a person, 54 by a machine turn). Judged
by the author first, against the registered classes:

| Class | Replies | What they are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N0, no naming | 50 | status lines (53 to about 900 characters: waiting for a run, acknowledging a task report or a message), and 4 longer replies |
| N1, names a decision that shaped the reply | 21 | every long finishing report (about 2,500 to 5,800 characters); 2 to 7 decisions each, each with its source and mostly a clause on how it applied |
| N2, names none or says none applied | 0 | |
| N3, names only decisions that did not shape it | 0 | |

Noise (N2 + N3) is 0 of 71, under the 10% bar: **the rule chooses (a).**

- *"Short ones included" is not borne out.* No status line carries the names. The one typed question answered
  without a change and under 1,500 characters carries none.
- *Under-naming, outside the rule:* one long final report after a build names no decision and cites "the rule" in
  passing. Of the 22 task-completion reports, 21 name their decisions.
- *Repetition, outside the rule:* the reports repeat the same 3 or 4 standing decisions, because those decisions
  govern most of that repo's work.

## Second opinion (Gemini 4 Argon, `agy -p`, plan mode; brief: the options, the registered check and the counts)

- *The check:*
  - the denominator holds status lines Claude never treats as finishing, so the line fired on 21 of 22 real
    task-completion reports;
  - N1 is judged per reply, so a list of seven with one real item still scores N1. Citation-level padding is
    unmeasured;
  - standing decisions repeated report after report are boilerplate to the reader even when each is N1.
- *The choice:* keep (a). Neither (b) nor (c) touches the repetition: all 21 named lists sit in turns that changed
  files or made a recommendation, and were shaped by standing decisions. The fix it names for repetition is a new
  option: (d) name only decisions not already named in this session. That keeps the fresh-session eval at its score.
- *Blind spots:*
  - one repo with a bimodal workflow (background runs, long reports), so short conversational Q&A turns are barely
    sampled;
  - one rater who knew the cost of (b) and (c), and judged leniently;
  - the no-change turns not split into typed questions and machine turns.
- *Risks and triggers it proposes:*
  - footer blindness, the same standing decisions trained into skipping;
  - under-naming after long builds (1 in 22);
  - triggers: short typed questions carrying names in 2 or more of 10; more than half of the decisions named after a
    session's first list being repeats; under-naming in 2 or more of 20 task reports.

## Weighing

- *Denominator:* granted. It does not move the choice, since the numerator is 0 either way. Over the 22
  task-completion reports, 21 name their decisions, which is item (5) working.
- *Citation-level padding and the lenient rater:* granted as gaps. An independent blind rating of all 71 replies, per
  reply and per named decision, was run to test them (below). It was added after the result, and the bar is unchanged.
- *Option (d), session-scoped naming:* not chosen now.
  - The value of the names is that each report can be audited alone. The peer's own review kept the closing names for
    that reason: they let the owner audit a report.
  - A report that leaves out a governing decision because an earlier report named it loses that.
  - After a compaction Claude cannot know what it named before, so "not already named" would be followed unevenly.
  - The repeated items are one line each.
  - (d) is recorded as the candidate if the repetition is shown to cost the reader.
- *Gemini's repetition trigger* (more than half of the names repeats) would fire on today's corpus as it stands. A
  trigger already true reopens nothing new, so the repetition is recorded as a known property, and the user's reading
  stays its trigger (D23's third).
- *Triggers taken:* short typed questions carrying names in 2 or more of 10, and under-naming in 2 or more of 20
  task-completion reports, both counted in the field.

## Independent rating (a blind subagent: given the line, the classes and the replies, not the options or the rule)

- *Per reply:* N0 49, N1 22, N2 0, N3 0. The one difference from the author: a long reply that names "research first"
  inline, counted N1 by the rater and N0 by the author. Noise stays 0 of 71, so the rule's choice holds.
- *Per named decision:* 109 named in the N1 replies, 99 applied (the reply shows the decision mattered in that turn), 10
  restated (named, with nothing in the reply showing it mattered: hero-id or probe-naming conventions in a research
  answer, a score rule in a turn that only corrected a summary, a general goal). That is 9%, spread over 7 of the 22
  lists. No reply names only restated decisions.
- *Short typed questions* (typed, no change, under about 1,500 characters): 2, a real question and a status line.
  Neither carries names. The one typed no-change reply that does (2,510 characters) names a decision it applied.
- *Under-naming:* the rater also reads the long final report after a build as naming nothing ("as the rule says").

## Choice: (a), the line stays frozen

- The registered rule chooses (a). The blind rating agrees on every class that decides it.
- (b) and (c) would cost a new build and a 10-run re-run to remove noise neither reader found. They would not touch
  the repetition, which is the cost the review most plausibly meant.
- *Recorded, not changed:*
  - the citation-level padding, 10 of 109 named decisions restated (9%): the baseline for a field trigger;
  - the repetition of standing decisions across reports, with (d), session-scoped naming, as the candidate if it is
    shown to cost the reader;
  - one under-naming miss in 22 task-completion reports.
- *New field triggers for D23:*
  - short typed questions carrying names in 2 or more of 10 counted;
  - under-naming in 2 or more of 20 task-completion reports;
  - restated decisions above 20% of those named in a field count of at least 20 lists.
